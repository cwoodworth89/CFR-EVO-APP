## The problem

ECOMM dispatches addresses that no City of Coquitlam address record holds. For those the geocoder
has no lot, so phase 1 shows the amber *location unresolved* card and phase 2 places the call by
arithmetic along a road segment's address range, under the *approximate location* banner.

On a block whose numbers rise evenly that lands well: 2973 Glen Dr came out 170 m along a 201 m
segment numbered 2951–2977, on Glen Drive in front of the right half of the building, and the
operator confirmed the placement. On an uneven block, or a building facing the other street, the
same arithmetic lands somewhere plausible and wrong, and nothing in the system can tell.

**2973 Glen Dr is a real address** (operator, 2026-09-13: *"it visibly has 2973 in streetview,
and most important ECOMM dispatches to that address"*). The City's own map labels carry it; its
address records do not.

## Evidence (measured 2026-09-13)

Every distinct operator-verified civic address in the dispatch corpus, **306**, checked against
every source that could be reached:

| Source | Holds it |
|:--|:--|
| Our copy of the City's Property Information (2025-06-22) and the City's live `Parcel_Addresses` | 275 both, 1 live only (629 Cottonwood Ave) |
| **In neither City address layer** | **30**, of which 6 are not civic addresses (2 Port Mann Bridge, 2 "block", 1 typo `3054 Loheed Hwy`, 1 Chartwell Rd — register §15) — **24 real** |

For the 24:

| Source | Has it as an address |
|:--|:--|
| City live `Parcel_Addresses` / the City's own locator (`Geocode/CoquitlamLocator`) | 0 / 0 |
| City Address Labels (a label point only, no street) | 1 (2973 Glen Dr) |
| Esri World Geocoder, as a point address | 4 — the other 20 Esri estimates along the street, as we do. A cloud service, so never a system source (CLAUDE.md §1) |

They cluster on the commercial strips: seven on Lougheed Hwy and three on Barnet Hwy. **About 8 %
of the distinct addresses dispatched are in no source this system can reach**; the address list
ECOMM dispatches from is the one that holds them.

## Operator rulings, 2026-09-13

1. **No alias** to a nearby City address: it would put crews at the wrong frontage under a number
   that was not dispatched.
2. **A person places these.** A human-placed arrival point is allowed for this purpose only, with
   a **named placer, a date and at least one piece of evidence** (a dispatch that used the address,
   the City label point, Street View). Coordinates generated by a script or a geocoder stay
   forbidden — the reason `custom_places` was removed (#7). The register's *"no hand-entered
   coordinates"* rule needs amending to say so when this is built.
3. **Phase 1 shows it immediately**, as it would a City lot, with a quiet *placed by the operator*
   note instead of the amber banner.
4. **CFR-owned data lives in a separate schema in the same database** (`cfr`), where a City
   re-import cannot reach it. See the backlog line on the parcel import for why that matters.
5. **Street names inferred from Address Labels are on hold.** A label point is a number and a
   position; taking the street from the lot it sits in names the wrong street in a measurable
   share of cases (17 of 64 label-only numbers exist as that number on another lot).
6. **Naming.** Not "verified" — `verified_*` is the HITL ground truth (`dispatches.verified_address`
   already exists). Not `custom_places` — the name of the dropped table. The name is open.

## Design (agreed shape)

* **A table in the `cfr` schema**, one row per address: house, street, suffix, the address as
  dispatched, status `pending` (on the review list, not placed) / `active` (placed) / `rejected`
  (not a real address, with the reason), arrival point, evidence (JSON, never empty), note,
  placed by, placed at.
* **Nothing derived is stored** (§6.6): no geometry column, no zone (computed from the point at
  read), no lot (the City has none, and borrowing a neighbour's would claim one), and no "the City
  has it now" flag — a view compares against `public.parcels` each time it is asked.
* **Geocoder:** a step after the exact City match and before the road-range estimate, scoring
  the street exactly as the exact-parcel step does. The City's record wins once it exists; the
  view lists the entry for retirement.
* **Phase 1:** treated as a place; the map grid is the zone of the placed point, labelled as
  derived, and phase 2 compares it with the spoken grid as it already does for a parcel's.
* **Review list:** seeded from the dispatch corpus (the 24 below), never auto-accepted; shown
  with the system's estimate and the City label point for reference; a person places the pin.
* **Upstream:** the list goes to the City GIS team and E-Comm, so the gap closes at its source.

## What would falsify it

* *A human-placed point beats the road-range estimate.* Measure: replay the dispatches that used
  each placed address and have the operator mark where crews actually stopped.
* *A placed address is found by the same street scoring as a City one.* Measure: run the corpus
  through the geocoder with the entries active and count calls that resolve to an entry on a
  different street than the verified column.
